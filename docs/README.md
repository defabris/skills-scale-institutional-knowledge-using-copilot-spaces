# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

This repository contains the core project management guidance used by OctoAcme to move work from idea to delivery and continuous improvement. The documentation is designed to help teams align on goals, ownership, communication, and quality expectations while reducing dependency on individual knowledge. Together, these process documents provide a practical framework for project initiation, planning, execution, release, risk management, and retrospective learning.

## Project management process overview

OctoAcme’s project lifecycle starts with initiation, when a new idea or feature is evaluated against the business problem, stakeholder needs, and measurable outcomes. Once there is alignment on the value to deliver, the team moves into planning, where the backlog is prioritized, milestones are defined, risks are captured, and estimates are created. During execution, work is tracked in a structured workflow with clear ownership, regular team syncs, defined quality gates, and active management of blockers and dependencies. Release and deployment follow a consistent process to ensure readiness, launch validation, and a documented rollback plan. After each milestone or sprint, the team conducts a retrospective to capture lessons learned and turn them into concrete improvements.

## Roles and communication model

The process docs emphasize clear roles and shared accountability across product, project, and engineering functions. Product managers define outcomes and prioritize value; project managers coordinate schedules, risks, communication, and documentation; developers build and validate solutions; QA supports acceptance and quality checks; and stakeholders provide input, decisions, and strategic alignment. OctoAcme relies on recurring communication rhythms such as daily standups, weekly planning or status reviews, milestone demos, and stakeholder updates to maintain transparency and help the team surface risks early. Escalation paths are defined so issues move from the team level to project leadership and then to sponsors when business impact warrants it.

## Quality and delivery expectations

Quality is embedded throughout the workflow rather than treated as a final checkpoint. The team uses unit tests, integration tests, smoke tests, CI validation, security scanning, and manual QA where appropriate to confirm that features meet acceptance criteria and operational requirements. Each project is expected to define a Definition of Done, manage dependencies explicitly, and maintain a risk register that is reviewed regularly. The docs also stress the importance of disciplined release management, including pre-release checks, deployment validation, rollback preparedness, and post-release communication.
